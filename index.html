About The Hackathon

Catalyzing India's Next Generation of Innovators

ZAYATHON is the signature innovation initiative by ZAYA CODE HUB, architected to transform classroom theory into high-impact, enterprise-grade technology prototypes.

Where Ambition Meets Execution

Hosted in collaboration with Sona College of Technology, Salem, ZAYATHON serves as a dynamic breeding ground for undergraduate engineering and computer science students (1st to 3rd year).

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about team eligibility, registration rules, hardware, judging, and prizes.

ZAYATHON is open to all passionate undergraduate college and university students (typically 1st, 2nd, and 3rd year). Students from any engineering, technology, computer applications, or science discipline are welcome to form cross-disciplinary teams.
To foster team collaboration, participants must register in teams of 2 to 4 members. You can appoint one Team Leader who completes the initial registration and lists the other teammates.
The 10 official tracks are: 1) Artificial Intelligence & Agentic AI, 2) Web3, Blockchain & DeFi, 3) FinTech & Digital Economy, 4) IoT & Smart Cities, 5) Cybersecurity & Threat Intelligence, 6) HealthTech & Bio-Informatics, 7) Robotics & Autonomous Systems, 8) Agritech & Rural Innovation, 9) Transportation & Smart Logistics, and 10) Open Innovation & Moonshots.
Click any "Register Now" button on this website to launch the 3-step interactive registration wizard. Enter your team details, leader & member information, choose your track, and submit. You will receive an instant digital team pass and verification ID.
ZAYATHON offers a premier hybrid experience. Shortlisted teams can attend in person at Sona College of Technology, Salem, Tamil Nadu, enjoying food, labs, swags, and round-the-clock venue access, or participate through the secure online submission stream.
Teams must submit: 1) A public GitHub repository with active commit history during the 48 hours, 2) A concise pitch deck (PDF/PPT), and 3) A 2–3 minute video demo or live prototype showing working features. Public open-source libraries are permitted with proper attribution.
Projects are scored across 5 core dimensions: Innovation & Originality (25%), Technical Complexity & Code Quality (25%), Practical Feasibility & Impact (20%), Completeness of Working Prototype (15%), and Clarity of Final Pitch & Defense (15%).
